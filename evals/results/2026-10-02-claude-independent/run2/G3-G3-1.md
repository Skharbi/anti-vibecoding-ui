I built the form as one self-contained file, `index.html`, in the working directory. I haven't opened it in a browser, so none of it has been tested.

I read "mobile insurance authorization" as a phone-first form where a patient lets a clinic bill their insurer. If you meant something else, such as prior authorization or mobile phone insurance, tell me and I'll rework the fields.

**What's on the form:**
1. **Patient:** name, date of birth, mobile phone, and optional email.
2. **Insurance:** insurer, member ID, optional group number, and who holds the policy. Choosing anyone other than "I do" reveals fields for the policyholder's name and date of birth.
3. **What you authorize:** plain-language terms (release of information, assignment of benefits, financial responsibility), two separate consent checkboxes, and a choice of how long the authorization lasts.
4. **Sign:** the patient types their full legal name, and today's date is added automatically.

After a successful submit, a confirmation screen shows a summary with the member ID masked. It also has print/save and start-over buttons.

**Mobile and accessibility choices:**
- Input text is 16px so iOS doesn't zoom on focus. Controls are at least 48px tall, and each radio or checkbox row is tappable as a whole.
- Phone, email, and name fields have the right keyboard types and autofill hints. Member and group IDs have autofill turned off.
- It reflows at 320px, respects safe-area insets, and follows the phone's light or dark setting.
- Each field has a visible label, hint text, and an inline error. A summary of errors appears at the top on a failed submit, and each entry jumps to its field.
- Errors always include text, not just a red border. Focus rings, reduced-motion settings, and screen-reader announcements for submit status are handled.
- Answers are never saved to local storage, because this is sensitive data. The form shows a failed-submit message and keeps what was typed so the user can retry.

**Before you use it:**

- The terms text is placeholder wording. Your compliance or legal team should approve the final text. For HIPAA-style authorizations, that includes how the revocation notice is worded.
- The signature is typed rather than drawn. It has to match the name entered in section 1.
- There is no clinic name or branding yet. I left that out so I didn't invent one.
