## Final output

I built the form as a single file, `index.html`, in the working directory. I haven't opened it in a browser, and I haven't run it through a screen reader or a keyboard-only pass.

I read "insurance authorization" as a patient authorizing a provider to bill their health plan and release records to it. If you meant something else, such as prior authorization or auto insurance, say so and I'll rework it.

**What the form covers**
- **Patient:** name, date of birth and mobile phone.
- **Health plan:** insurance company, member ID, optional group number, and who the policyholder is. The policyholder's name and date of birth only appear when it isn't the patient.
- **What you're authorizing:** billing and records release are required. Letting someone else discuss claims is optional. A dropdown sets how long the authorization lasts, and a "Things to know" section explains revoking it.
- **Sign:** typed full legal name, which must match the patient name. There is an option to sign as a parent, guardian or representative. The date is filled in automatically.

**Mobile behaviour**
- It's one column with 48px tap targets and 16px input text, so iOS doesn't zoom on focus.
- The submit button stays pinned to the bottom of the screen.
- Fields use the right keyboards and autofill hints. Member ID and group number have autofill and autocorrect turned off.
- Errors appear next to each field and in a summary at the top. Each item in the summary jumps to its field.
- On submit, the button shows a pending state and double-submits are blocked. A failed submit keeps the answers and offers "Try again". Success shows a read-back with the member ID masked to its last four characters.

**Gaps and caveats**
- **No backend:** submit is a stub with a 700ms delay and sends nothing. The comment in the code says what a real server must do: re-validate, authenticate, and record the signer, time and form version.
- **Legal wording:** the authorization text is placeholder copy, not reviewed legal language. The form itself tells the provider to have their compliance contact review it before use, and the wording and required fields vary by jurisdiction. I also didn't name a provider, so the copy says "your provider".
- **Typed signature:** it may not meet your legal requirements for an e-signature.
- **Browser compatibility:** the checkbox border highlight uses `:has()`, so it won't show on older browsers. The form still works without it.
